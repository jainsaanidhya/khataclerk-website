Invoice automation is the use of software to capture information from invoices, organise it into useful fields, and prepare it for an accounting workflow. Instead of retyping every supplier bill, a team can upload a document, review the extracted details, and send approved information to its accounting system.

## What is invoice automation?

## Why can manual invoice processing take time?

Supplier invoices arrive in different formats: PDFs, scans, photos, or paper documents. Someone may need to locate the vendor, invoice number, date, line items, tax amounts, and total, then enter those details into accounting software. Repeating these steps across many bills can slow bookkeeping and make it harder to spot a missing or inconsistent value.

Automation can reduce repetitive typing, but it does not remove the need for sensible checks. A blurry scan, unusual layout, or ambiguous item description may need a person to review it.

## How does invoice automation work?

An invoice processing workflow commonly has these stages:

1. **Capture:** Collect a PDF, image, scan, or other supported document.
2. **Extract:** OCR (optical character recognition) and document-processing tools identify text and organise fields such as supplier, invoice date, line items, and tax values.
3. **Review:** A person checks uncertain or important fields and corrects them when needed.
4. **Prepare:** The approved data is mapped to the accounting categories or ledgers used by the business.
5. **Post or export:** The entry is sent to, or prepared for, the accounting system according to the software and workflow in use.

The exact steps vary by product. In particular, “extraction” means reading information from a document; it does not by itself prove that a tax detail is correct or that an invoice has been approved for payment.

## A practical example

Imagine a distributor receives a supplier bill as a PDF. The accounts team uploads it, checks the extracted vendor, date, invoice number, item lines, and GST amounts, then confirms the appropriate ledger mapping. Once reviewed, the entry can be prepared for the business’s accounting workflow. If a field is unclear or the document is incomplete, the team can resolve it before posting rather than relying on an unchecked guess.

## Benefits and limitations

Automation may reduce repeated data entry, help teams process batches more consistently, and make extracted fields easier to review. Results still depend on document quality, supplier formats, field mapping, and the review process. Teams should understand how exceptions are flagged, who approves entries, and what “validation” means before relying on a workflow for accounting decisions.

## What should you consider when choosing software?

- Which documents and file formats can it process?
- Which invoice fields does it extract, and can users correct them?
- How does it handle GST-related fields and uncertain values?
- Does it connect to your accounting system, and is the workflow posting, export, or another method?
- Can your team review entries and control ledger mappings before they are sent?
- What happens when an invoice cannot be read or does not match your usual process?

Choose a tool based on your actual invoice volume, accounting setup, and controls. Confirm integration details with the provider and test representative documents before moving a large workflow.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is invoice OCR the same as invoice automation?

No. OCR reads text from a document. Invoice automation can use OCR as one part of a larger process that also includes review, mapping, and sending or exporting data.

### Can invoice automation process GST invoices?

Some tools extract GST-related fields when those values are present in a document. Check exactly which fields are read and what, if anything, is validated; extraction alone is not a guarantee of tax compliance.

### Does invoice automation automatically enter bills into Tally?

That depends on the product and its Tally workflow. Check whether the tool supports the relevant Tally version, how it connects, and whether a person reviews the entry before it is posted. KhataClerk’s [Tally tutorial](/tutorial/) describes its setup and review flow.
